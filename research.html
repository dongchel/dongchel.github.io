Macroscopic Quantum Mechanics

Bringing mechanical objects heavy enough to feel gravity into the quantum regime, using quantum-limited optical measurement and feedback.

Laser cooling a centimeter-scale torsion pendulum

Torsion pendula have long been the instrument of choice for measuring weak gravitational forces, and they keep a high mechanical quality factor even when mass-loaded. We laser-cooled a centimeter-scale torsional oscillator from room temperature to 10 mK, an average occupancy of 6000 phonons, using radiation-pressure forces conditioned on a quantum-noise-limited optical measurement.

The readout is a “mirrored” optical lever that passively rejects spatial-mode noise by 60 dB, reaching an imprecision 9.8 dB below the peak zero-point motion. Together with a mechanical quality factor of 1.4 × 107, this demonstrates the ingredients needed to reach the quantum ground state of torsional motion.

D.-C. Shin, T. M. Hayward, D. Fife, R. Menon, V. Sudhir · Optica 12, 473 (2025)

Theoretical Tools for Quantum Metrology

Quantum theory for predicting, and beating, the noise limits of optical measurements.

Continuum-field quantum optics of frequency comb metrology

Every comb-based measurement ends at a photodetector, where the quantum noise of light sets the ultimate limit. We developed a first-principles quantum theory that traces this noise to the fluctuations surrounding each comb line, built on continuous-mode field quantization.

The framework unifies the standard quantum limits of optical frequency division and dual-comb spectroscopy, and identifies resource-efficient routes beyond them using engineered comb states such as squeezed and entangled light.

D.-C. Shin, E. Ng, M.-G. Suh, V. Sudhir · arXiv:2605.16702 (2026)

Frequency Comb Metrology

Using optical frequency combs to transfer optical-cavity stability to terahertz frequencies and to synchronize independent combs.

Ultra-stable terahertz synthesis from an optical frequency comb

Precise terahertz spectroscopy, radar and wireless links all need stable terahertz sources. We brought optical-comb stabilization down to the terahertz region: a comb locked to an ultra-low-expansion optical cavity supplies pairs of comb lines that are photomixed to generate frequencies from 0.10 to 1.10 THz.

At 0.66 THz, the frequency instability is 4.4 × 10−15 at 1 s, averaging down to 5.1 × 10−17 at 65 s, with a phase noise of −70 dBc/Hz at 1 Hz offset.

D.-C. Shin, B. S. Kim, H. Jang, Y.-J. Kim, S.-W. Kim · Nature Communications 14, 790 (2023)

Compact all-fiber platform for 1-Hz-linewidth comb synchronization

Dual-comb spectroscopy and ranging require high mutual coherence between two combs, which usually means referencing both to ultra-stable cavity lasers. Instead, we extract two comb lines by injection-locking off-the-shelf diode lasers and use them to phase-lock a second comb, in a compact all-fiber platform.

The residual timing jitter between the two pulse trains is 37 fs, and multi-heterodyne measurements resolve 1-Hz linewidths between the combs: a mutual coherence time of over 1 s, comparable to that of ultra-stable lasers.

D.-C. Shin†, J. Yang†, D. I. Lee, G. Kang, S.-W. Kim, Y.-J. Kim · Optics & Laser Technology 203, 115526 (2026) · †Equal contribution
